01 — Problem

Nothing was built for this.

PMDD affects roughly 3–8% of people who menstruate — yet every tracking app on the market treats it as a footnote to general period tracking. Clinical dashboards, sterile UIs, and symptom lists designed for doctors, not for the person living through it.

As someone managing PMDD myself, I needed something that understood the emotional and cognitive weight of the luteal phase. Something that made logging feel safe, not like filling out a medical form. So I built it.

"The best tools are the ones you build because nothing else fits."

02 — Insight

Track the pattern, not just the pain.

01

Emotional safety first

Logging symptoms should never feel like a clinical intake form. The language, hierarchy, and visual tone were designed to feel like a personal journal — warm, private, yours.

02

Zero data leaves your device

Health data is deeply personal. No accounts, no servers, no tracking. Everything lives in localStorage — a deliberate architectural decision that is itself a design statement.

03

The luteal phase is the story

PMDD symptoms emerge in the 1–2 weeks before menstruation. The app was built around surfacing that window — helping users see the connection between their cycle phase and their emotional state.

04

Streaks as motivation, not pressure

Consistent logging is what generates meaningful insight. But tracking streaks when you're having a hard day adds guilt. The design treats streaks as encouragement, never punishment.

03 — Features

Everything it does.

01 Daily Check-in Mood on a 1–10 scale, energy slider, emotional and physical symptom selection, period status and flow intensity, free-write journal reflection, and a gratitude note. Everything in one session.
02 Calendar View Every logged day color-coded by mood at a glance. Good days, neutral days, hard days, and period days each have a distinct visual treatment. Click any day to see the full entry.
03 Insights Dashboard Total entries logged, average mood, current streak, period days tracked, mood-over-time chart, most frequent symptoms, and energy trends over the last 30 days.
04 Cycle Tracker Log cycle start and end dates, view cycle history, and see a luteal phase guide that contextualizes when PMDD symptoms are most likely to emerge within your personal cycle.
05 Full Privacy No sign-up, no server, no analytics. All data stored locally in the browser via localStorage. The app works offline. Your health data is entirely yours.

05 — Design Decisions

Why it works the way it does.

Privacy by architecture

No server. No account. No leaks.

The decision to store everything in localStorage wasn't a technical shortcut — it was a design statement. Health data, especially around mental health and menstruation, is deeply stigmatized. Users needed to trust the tool before they'd use it honestly.

Language & tone

Soft where clinical would be cold.

Every label, prompt, and empty state was written to feel like a journal, not a form. "How are you feeling?" instead of "Rate your mood." "One thing I'm grateful for" instead of "Notes." The words do as much design work as the layout.

Color as communication

The calendar tells the story at a glance.

Color-coding in the calendar view — good days, neutral days, hard days, period days — lets users see their cycle patterns without reading a single number. Pattern recognition is the whole point.

Gratitude as a feature

Ending on something good.

Ending each daily check-in with a gratitude note is deliberate. On hard days, it's grounding. On good days, it's amplifying. It shifts the emotional register of the whole logging session.
